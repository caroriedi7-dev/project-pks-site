Searching for ways to act on Pallister-Killian Syndrome.

For Giulia and for all children affected by Pallister-Killian Syndrome.

Giulia has Pallister-Killian Syndrome, a rare genetic disorder for which there is currently no targeted treatment.

With a specialised scientific team, we want to create brain models from the cells of children with PKS, in order to understand the disease and search for therapeutic avenues.

The scientific protocol is defined. The lab is ready.

What's missing now is the funding needed to launch the first phase of the programme.

We decided not to wait

When Giulia was diagnosed, we discovered that very little research existed on the mechanisms of Pallister-Killian Syndrome — and even fewer programmes genuinely looking for treatment avenues.

So we decided not to wait for a project to appear one day.

Together with specialised researchers, we built a scientific programme to create the first models needed to understand what happens in the brain cells of children with PKS and to test therapeutic strategies.

This project began for Giulia, but its results could benefit all children and families affected by the syndrome.

A first phase to understand and test

Brain organoids are small cellular models grown in the lab from patients' cells. They make it possible to study certain stages of nerve-cell development and the biological effects of PKS.

1

Create the models

Transform the cells of three children into stem cells, then into brain organoids.

2

Observe

Study the development, organisation and functioning of nerve cells.

3

Compare

Compare PKS models with control models to identify the alterations linked to the disease.

4

Measure

Define biological markers to objectively assess the observed effects.

5

Test

Evaluate substances and different scientific strategies to select the most promising avenues.

The expected results

This first phase should move us from still-limited knowledge of PKS to results that can be directly used to guide therapeutic research.

The most important mechanisms identified

Determine which biological anomalies play a major role in the effects of PKS on nerve cells.

Therapeutic targets and avenues identified

Identify the genes, proteins or mechanisms that it might be possible to act on.

The most promising approaches selected

Compare the effects of the different strategies tested to determine which ones deserve to be pursued further.

The Project PKS association

An association created to bring about and fund research into Pallister-Killian Syndrome.

Our mission

Project PKS exists to launch and fund research specifically dedicated to Pallister-Killian Syndrome.

Our goal is to understand which biological mechanisms are responsible for the difficulties caused by PKS, to identify the targets it might be possible to act on, and to test therapeutic avenues on models derived from children with the syndrome.

Our ambition is clear: to turn knowledge about PKS into concrete avenues that can open new prospects for children.

Frequently asked questions

▾ How is the programme funded?
Project PKS seeks funding from several sources: individual donors, companies, foundations and major donors. The detailed scientific and budget dossier is shared as part of sponsorship and funding requests.
▾ Can a small donation really help?
Yes. No single donation funds a full experiment on its own, but every contribution strengthens the campaign, shows that a community supports the programme, and makes it easier to secure larger funding.
▾ Is a treatment guaranteed?
No. This first phase is meant to understand the mechanisms of the disease and to evaluate avenues. It may identify promising options, but it may also show that certain strategies do not work.
▾ How can I follow how donations are used?
Project PKS will publish the programme's progress, the steps taken and the available financial information. Companies and major donors can also request the detailed documents.
